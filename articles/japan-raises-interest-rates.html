Japan's central bank, the Bank of Japan (BoJ), increased its target interest rate to 1.25%, the highest level in 31 years, to address rising inflation. This move comes as central banks worldwide have raised interest rates in response to high energy prices driving up inflation. The BoJ had held rates for the sixth time in a row before this increase, but the Bank of England predicts a future rise is more likely due to ongoing high energy prices.

What Happened

The Bank of Japan (BoJ) voted to raise its target interest rate from 1% to 1.25%, marking the highest level since 1995. This decision follows similar actions by other major central banks, such as the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank, which have been tightening monetary policy in response to global inflation. The BoJ's move is seen as a response to the war in Iran, which has pushed up energy prices and, consequently, inflation in Japan.

Why It Matters

The increase in interest rates is aimed at curbing inflation, which has been driven by high energy prices. This move by the BoJ is part of a broader global trend where central banks are raising interest rates to control inflation. The Bank of England's prediction of a future rise in interest rates highlights the ongoing challenges central banks face in managing inflationary pressures. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's campaign for higher rates added to the pressure on the BoJ to act.

Background

Central banks around the world have been raising interest rates to combat inflation, which has been exacerbated by high energy prices. The Bank of Japan had held its rates for the sixth consecutive meeting, but the pressure to act has increased due to the global economic climate. The war in Iran, which has led to higher energy prices, has had a significant impact on Japan's economy, prompting the BoJ to take action. The situation is not unique to Japan; many countries are facing similar challenges, as energy prices have surged globally, affecting economies and central bank policies.
