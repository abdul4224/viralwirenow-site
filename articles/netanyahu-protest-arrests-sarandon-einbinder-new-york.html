Key takeaways

  • Actors Susan Sarandon and Hannah Einbinder were detained during a demonstration opposing Benjamin Netanyahu in New York.
  • The demonstration, organized by Jewish Voice for Peace, coincided with the Israeli prime minister's appearance at the United Nations General Assembly.
  • More than 100 people were arrested during protests surrounding the visit, according to Al Jazeera.

Police in New York moved in to disperse crowds on Thursday, arresting more than 100 people across the city as demonstrations surged against the arrival of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Among those taken into custody were prominent cultural figures, including Academy Award-winning actor Susan Sarandon and television performer Hannah Einbinder.

The demonstrations erupted directly alongside the annual convening of the United Nations General Assembly, where world leaders assemble to address the international diplomatic body. As Netanyahu prepared to deliver his speech from the assembly rostrum, demonstrators took to the city's streets to register direct opposition to his presence and policies, drawing a forceful response from local law enforcement.

Detentions at the Jewish Voice for Peace Demonstration

The specific demonstration where the actors were apprehended was organized by the activist organization Jewish Voice for Peace. The group assembled demonstrators in New York to publicly challenge the Israeli leader’s trip and his official appearance before international delegates at the UN headquarters.

Video evidence from the scene documented the direct intervention by law enforcement. Footage captured by Agence France-Presse showed New York Police Department officers placing Sarandon—widely recognized for her starring role in Thelma & Louise—into handcuffs as officers dispersed the gathering. Einbinder, known for her role in the television series Hacks, was likewise detained by officers on the scene as the assembly was cleared.

The presence of widely known public figures at grassroots actions often amplifies visibility for the underlying cause. In this instance, the detention of Sarandon and Einbinder drew immediate global attention to the Jewish Voice for Peace demonstration, highlighting the intensity of public dissent aimed at foreign heads of government speaking on American soil.

Citywide Enforcement and Over 100 Arrests

The detentions of Sarandon and Einbinder were part of a much larger law enforcement operation across New York on Thursday. According to Al Jazeera, police arrested more than 100 individuals who had joined street protests against Netanyahu’s visit to the United Nations.

During the UN General Assembly, New York routinely implements extensive security perimeters, deploying substantial police resources to manage traffic, secure motorcades, and regulate public access around diplomatic compounds. When demonstrations convene in close proximity to official venues, law enforcement frequently issues dispersal warnings before executing mass arrests to maintain open corridors and prevent disruptions to diplomatic movements.

Local coverage documented the direct clash between international diplomacy and municipal security operations. ABC7 New York reported that the Israeli prime minister addressed the United Nations General Assembly against the backdrop of these active protests, demonstrating the sharp divide between the secure assembly floor and the disputed public spaces outside.

Competing Gatherings Outside the Assembly

The streets outside the international complex saw multiple viewpoints converge during the diplomatic summit. While Jewish Voice for Peace and other groups mobilized against Netanyahu's policy and rhetoric, other demonstrators gathered nearby with an entirely different mandate.

According to Spectrum News NY1, a separate rally assembled outside the UN facility to urge gathered world leaders to confront antisemitism directly. This gathering emphasized the need for international representatives convening at the General Assembly to take an explicit stand against discrimination and hate directed at Jewish communities globally.

This juxtaposition turned Midtown into a focal point of intense political expression. The New York Times characterized Netanyahu's UN address as a "high-stakes speech," reflecting both the contentious diplomatic environment inside the legislative hall and the volatile public sentiment manifest across city blocks just beyond the security checkpoints.
